01 · THE MARKET UNIVERSE

Understanding Market Instruments

A financial instrument is an asset or contract that can be traded or invested in. Each instrument has its own market structure, price drivers, trading hours, volatility, and risks.

Understanding the instrument is the first step toward making informed decisions. A strategy that works in one market may behave differently in another.

KEY INSIGHT

Before looking for an entry, understand what you are trading, how its price moves, and which risks may affect it.

02 · THE MAIN INSTRUMENTS

Markets Every Trader Should Know

These markets are commonly followed by traders and investors. Their characteristics are different, so each requires its own analysis.

01 / FOREX

Foreign Exchange

Forex is the market for exchanging one currency for another. Currency pairs such as EUR/USD express the value of one currency relative to another. Interest rates, economic data, and central-bank decisions can influence exchange rates.

02 / GOLD

Gold · XAU/USD

Gold is a precious metal traded globally. Its price can respond to US dollar movements, real yields, interest-rate expectations, investment demand, and geopolitical uncertainty. Gold can be highly volatile, especially around major economic releases.

03 / INDICES

Stock Market Indices

An index measures the performance of a group of securities under a defined methodology. Examples include the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100. Index prices can be influenced by constituent companies, earnings, interest rates, and overall market sentiment.

04 / EQUITIES

Individual Stocks

Equities represent ownership in a company. Their prices may respond to earnings, business performance, company announcements, valuation, and broader economic conditions. Individual stocks can carry company-specific risks.

05 / CRYPTO

Cryptocurrency

Cryptocurrencies are digital assets that use cryptographic technologies. Bitcoin and Ether are prominent examples. Prices may react to adoption, liquidity, regulation, market sentiment, and developments in the crypto ecosystem. Volatility can be substantial.

06 / DERIVATIVES

CFDs and Other Derivatives

A derivative derives its value from an underlying asset or reference price. A Contract for Difference (CFD), where available, allows traders to speculate on price movements without owning the underlying asset. CFDs may involve leverage, financing costs, and significant risk of loss.

03 · COMPARE BEFORE YOU TRADE

Different Instruments, Different Risks

No instrument is always the best choice. The right instrument depends on your knowledge, trading objectives, risk tolerance, and the conditions of the market.

Instrument Potential Price Drivers Key Considerations
Forex Interest rates, economic data, currency demand Leverage, spreads, event volatility
Gold US dollar, yields, investment demand Sharp moves, news sensitivity
Indices Corporate earnings, rates, sentiment Broad market sell-offs, concentration
Equities Company results, outlook, valuation Company-specific events, liquidity
Cryptocurrency Adoption, liquidity, regulation, sentiment High volatility, custody, platform risk
RISK REMINDER

Leverage magnifies exposure and can magnify losses. Product terms, trading hours, spreads, and available protections vary by provider and jurisdiction. Understand the terms before placing a trade.

04 · BUILD YOUR MARKET CHECKLIST

Before Opening a Position

Use these checks to avoid choosing an instrument based only on excitement or a recent price move.

01
Understand the Product

Know whether you are trading an asset, a share, a currency pair, or a derivative.

02
Identify the Main Drivers

Know which economic releases, company events, or market conditions may affect the price.

03
Check Trading Conditions

Review spreads, fees, trading hours, liquidity, leverage, and contract specifications.

04
Define Your Risk

Set your invalidation level and position size before entering the market.
